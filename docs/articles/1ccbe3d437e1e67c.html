Technical Details

At least 178 top web destinations unwittingly served a variety of malware exploits against visitors over the past 24 hours thanks to a security vulnerability involving Optimizely , a company that helps websites customize user experiences and test out new features. Report URI researcher Scott Helme wrote that the payload delivered by the malicious Javascript was rebuilt 26 times during the day, dropping a multi-purpose malware kit that could steal payment card data, cookies and screenshots, and swap any cryptocurrency payment addresses for the attacker s. Contacted for comment, Optimizely said that on November 16, 2026 it identified a security incident in which an unauthorized party exploited a vulnerability in the Optimizely Experimentation to add malicious JavaScript to some customer projects. According to Report URI, 68 of the sites had no CSP at all, while 50 of them had a CSP that still allowed the malware payload and the data theft.

Impact

This means that this supply chain attack didn t require any clicking or consent to trigger most of the impacted clients would immediately serve up the payload as soon as someone loaded the page, further increasing risks from this type of JavaScript snippet being compromised. Helme said he hopes this incident will encourage more sites to adopt effective CSPs, noting that it could have been far worse: By Report URI s estimates, the purveyors of this malware attack managed to steal only around $7,500 worth of virtual currency from affected users, as well as an unknown number of payment card records. A website can be compromised through a third-party dependency without its own code or infrastructure ever being breached.

Assessed severity: HIGH.

Mitigation

These measures included blocking access to the vulnerable path, deploying a fix, removing the injected code from affected projects, ending active sessions, revoking tokens, and resetting access for affected user accounts. These days, your ad/script blocker is your firewall. Experts say the incident, which saw data-stealing malicious software silently injected into sites like the BBC , CNN ,  Equifax , Ledger and Zoom , was exacerbated by the reality that few popular websites enforce strict rules on the sources of third-party content.
